## Historical scholarship on mathematical transcendence

These are secondary histories; link them, but do not commit copyrighted modern books.

1. **Viktor Blåsjö, *Transcendental Curves in the Leibnizian Calculus* (Academic Press, 2017).** [Publisher](https://shop.elsevier.com/books/transcendental-curves-in-the-leibnizian-calculus/blasjo/978-0-12-813237-1). Particularly relevant to constructions of the catenary, tractrix, exponential curves, and the calculus.
2. **Michel Serfati, *Leibniz and the Invention of Mathematical Transcendence* (Franz Steiner Verlag, 2018).** [Publisher](https://www.steiner-verlag.de/en/Leibniz-and-the-Invention-of-Mathematical-Transcendence/9783515120821). Especially close to the precise historical question “what did Leibniz mean by transcendence?”.
3. **Jesper Lützen, *A History of Mathematical Impossibility* (Oxford University Press, 2023).** [Oxford publisher](https://academic.oup.com/book/45622). Chapter 8, *Circle Quadrature in the Seventeenth Century*, studies the Leibniz/Newton context and impossibility arguments. This is **not a book exclusively about Leibniz or transcendentals**, but may be the Lützen book remembered.
4. **Jesper Lützen**, “Between Rigor and Applications: Developments in the Concept of Function in Mathematical Analysis,” in *The Cambridge History of Science*, vol. 5, a historical treatment of changes in “function.” [Cambridge University Press](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-history-of-science/between-rigor-and-applications-developments-in-the-concept-of-function-in-mathematical-analysis/402C4C6CD65B87B0A1083219B36F3D8D).
5. **Davide Crippa, *The Impossibility of Squaring the Circle in the 17th Century: A Debate Among Gregory, Huygens and Leibniz* (Birkhäuser, 2019).** [Publisher and chapter list](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-01638-8). A full-length book particularly close to Leibniz, algebraic constructibility, and transcendental functions. **Jesper Lützen reviewed this book in *Historia Mathematica* 52 (2020)** — another plausible source of the recalled Lützen connection.
6. **Historical research article**, [*The Leibniz catenary and approximation of e — an analysis of his unpublished calculations*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.hm.2019.06.001), *Historia Mathematica* (2019). Do not confuse unpublished manuscripts reconstructed by historians with the two actual 1691 journal papers.

## Citation and reading discipline

For each claimed historical lineage (e.g. “Leibniz → Cotes → Gauss → Riemann”):

- Capture a specific passage **in each author's own text**.
- Record which part of the chain is a demonstrable quotation/citation, which is mathematical similarity, and which is later historiographical inference.
- If an essay presents a modern formula as if it appeared in an original seventeenth-century paper, record the original symbolic statement and the coordinate transformation that produces the modern formula.
- Maintain original language vs modern translation as separate evidence.
- Compare the algebra, geometry, limiting operation and topology on small worked examples rather than asking readers to accept a thematic claim.

A bibliography records evidence and uncertainties; it does not ratify Director's or Tennenbaum's interpretations.
