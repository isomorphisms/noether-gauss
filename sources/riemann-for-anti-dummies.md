# Bruce Director — *Riemann for Anti-Dummies*

**Type:** a numbered series of pedagogical articles and historical interpretations, by Bruce Director, published in the LaRouche movement's educational publications. **Not** an edition of Riemann's original writings.

The archive includes a Part 69. **Do not infer that every number below 69 has been individually checked or is still accessible.** LYM Canada's modern posting dates can differ from the original article dates.

## Connection to the orbit of Ceres

- [Jonathan Tennenbaum and Bruce Director, “How Gauss Determined the Orbit of Ceres”](https://archive.schillerinstitute.com/fid_97-01/982_Gauss_Ceres.html), *Fidelio* VII, no. 2, Summer 1998. Publisher's index, full PDF, and chapter links. **Both are credited as authors.**
- [“Why We Should All Understand Gauss”](https://21sci-tech.com/articles/Summer03/GaussUnderstanding.html), *21st Century Science & Technology*, Summer 2003. Contemporary discussion of the use of Director's *Riemann for Anti-Dummies* series in studying Gauss's work.

## Suggested reconstruction tasks

1. **Ceres:** Read the Tennenbaum–Director paper and reconstruct the orbit calculation from observations. Record the observations actually supplied, computational assumptions, and comparison with Gauss's published method.
2. **Algebra:** Compare Part 27 with Gauss's 1799 dissertation, carefully separating its mathematical argument from Director's historical interpretation.
3. **Geometry:** Work from Parts 45 and 48 to Gauss's original surface-curvature and conformal-mapping papers. Recreate the diagrams computationally.
4. **Riemann:** Use Parts 58, 60, 61, 64, 66, and 67 as prompts to examine original texts on the Dirichlet principle, branched surfaces, Abelian functions, and hypergeometric functions.

**Provenance rule:** record the original author's words separately from quotations or historical claims embedded in Director's essays. Trace claims to Gauss, Riemann, or original observations before treating them as established. This series is a secondary source for nineteenth-century mathematics.
